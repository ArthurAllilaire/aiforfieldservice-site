AI for field service: what it does and how to choose

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

AI for field service is no longer one thing. Some of it is built into the field service suite you already pay for, some is a separate layer on top, and the two do different jobs. This page explains what the technology does today, using what 12 vendors publish about their own products.

Key takeaways

  • AI in field service does six jobs: it briefs the technician, helps find the fault, writes the report, handles parts and quotes, plans the schedule and shows leaders what is going on.
  • What matters most is where the answers come from: the records in one system, your documents, or the history of each asset linked together.
  • Few vendors publish a price. Of the 12 we checked, three do.
  • No vendor page we checked says its AI features work offline.
  • The only test that tells you anything is a pilot on your own jobs, measured against a number you already track.

What is AI for field service?

AI for field service is software that reads a service company's records, manuals and job history and uses them to help the people doing the work. In 2026 that mostly means a language model connected to a field service system. It answers a technician's question, drafts the paperwork or prepares the next job.

It is sold in two forms. Suite AI is a feature of the field service system itself, such as Agentforce in Salesforce or Copilot in Dynamics 365. Specialist AI is a separate product that connects to the systems you run and answers from more than one of them.

What AI does in field service today

These are the six jobs vendors describe, with the products that document each one. The longer version, with limits, is on the use cases page.

Briefs the technician before the job

It pulls the customer, the asset and the last visits into a short brief. Salesforce, IFS, SAP, ServiceMax and Simpro all describe one.

Helps find the fault

It guides troubleshooting from manuals, past cases and the history of the asset. Aquant, Neuron7 and Kenner are built around this, and Salesforce lists it for Agentforce. Microsoft's documented Copilot features do not include it.

Writes the report

It turns what the technician did or said into the work order summary. Almost every suite drafts one. Kenner and Simpro Lightning write it from the technician speaking.

Handles parts and quotes

Oracle's start-of-day brief reserves parts. Kenner orders parts and prepares quotes through the CRM and ERP.

Plans and schedules

Scheduling and dispatch optimisation lives in the field service suites. None of the specialist tools we cite describes doing it.

Shows leaders what is going on

Aquant gives analytics on teams, assets and parts. Kenner reports what breaks across units, which faults take longest to diagnose, and first-time fix by team, model and site.

Where the answers come from

Every one of these tools is only as useful as what it reads. There are three kinds of source, and a vendor should be able to tell you which it uses.

The records in one system

Work orders, bookings and assets in the suite. This is what ServiceNow, ServiceMax and Dynamics 365 describe. It is enough for a brief or a summary.

Your documents

Manuals, bulletins and knowledge base articles. Oracle's Field Assist answers from the knowledge base. MaintainX Assist answers from asset manuals and work orders and cites its sources. SAP's Joule answers from SAP's own product documentation.

The history of each asset, linked together

Past jobs, alarms, parts and manuals connected to the unit they belong to. Kenner calls this a knowledge graph, built on top of all the systems a company runs. Neuron7 describes a graph built from manuals, case history, tickets and technician notes.

What it costs

Most vendors quote on request. On the pages we checked, Salesforce lists Agentforce for Field Service at $125 per user per month on top of its Field Service licences. Microsoft includes Copilot in the $105 per user per month Dynamics 365 Field Service licence. MaintainX has plans from free to $65 per user per month. Kenner, which we make, is free while in beta.

IFS needs its IFS.ai Activation Pass or a token add-on, and SAP treats most of its generative features as premium. The cost page lists what each price includes.

Limits and risks

It is only as good as the records

Oracle's answers come from the customer's knowledge base. MaintainX says its answers depend on how complete the work orders, comments and notes are. If the old reports say "repaired unit", there is nothing to learn from.

It can be wrong

A language model can state a wrong answer with confidence. Treat any answer about a specific model as something to check against the manual, and prefer tools that show where the answer came from, as MaintainX Assist and Kenner say they do.

It may not work where the work is

Several vendors say their mobile app works offline. None of the pages we checked says the AI features themselves do.

Some of it is not finished

Microsoft's AI work order updates are still a preview feature. Ask every vendor what is generally available today.

People have to use it

A tool that needs an app opened and a question typed gets used in the van afterwards, if at all. Check how a technician reaches it with gloves on.

How to start

  1. Pick one problem you already measure, such as first-time fix, callbacks or the time technicians spend on reports.
  2. Find out where the knowledge for that problem lives: in the suite, in manuals, or in old work orders and a few senior heads.
  3. Run a short pilot with one team on your own jobs. Two weeks with five to ten technicians is enough to see whether they use it.
  4. Compare the number before and after, then decide whether to roll it out.

How to test a tool before you buy

Use ten of your own bad jobs

Take recent callbacks, second visits and escalations. Ask the tool what it would have told the technician. A demo on the vendor's data tells you little.

Test it where the work happens

On a roof, in a plant room, with gloves on and a weak signal. If it needs both hands and a full screen, it will not be used.

Follow one answer to the end

Check that the tool shows where its answer came from, and that the note or report lands in your system of record without retyping.

Questions and answers

What is AI in field service management?

It is software that uses a service company's own records, manuals and job history to help with the work: briefing the technician, guiding the repair, writing the report, and planning the schedule. It is sold either as part of a field service suite or as a specialist product that connects to one.

How is AI used in field service?

In six ways today: briefs before the job, troubleshooting help during it, the report afterwards, parts and quotes, scheduling and dispatch, and analytics for service leaders. Most products do two or three of these, not all six.

What are the benefits of AI for field service?

Vendors point to fewer repeat visits, less time on paperwork and faster training of new technicians. None of that is guaranteed. Measure it on your own jobs against a number you already track.

What are the challenges of AI in field service?

Incomplete records, answers that can be wrong, features that need a connection, add-on licences, and technicians who will not open another app. Each is covered under limits and risks above.

Is AI already built into my field service software?

Probably. Salesforce, Microsoft, ServiceNow, IFS, SAP, Oracle, ServiceMax, MaintainX and Simpro all describe AI features. Check which plan or add-on includes them before you plan around it.

Aquant vs Neuron7: what is the difference?

Both guide technicians to the fix. Aquant reaches them by voice call, SMS, web or the phone camera and positions itself for companies that make and service their own equipment. Neuron7 sits inside the CRM or field service system and says most of its customers have 1,000 or more technicians.

Agentforce vs Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service: what is the difference?

Salesforce lists Agentforce for Field Service at $125 per user per month on top of its licences and describes briefs, summaries and troubleshooting. Microsoft includes Copilot in the $105 licence and documents summaries, record updates and data questions, with no troubleshooting guidance.
