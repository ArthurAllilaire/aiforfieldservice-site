AI for Field Service

AI use cases in field service, with examples

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

The short answer

Follow one service visit from the booking to the invoice and there are four places AI now helps: before the technician leaves, on site, after the job, and in the office. The examples below come from the vendors' own pages.

  • Kenner: troubleshooting from the unit's history, the report, parts and quotes. Our product.
  • Aquant: troubleshooting guidance by voice, text and phone camera.
  • Salesforce Agentforce for Field Service: a pre-work brief, troubleshooting and a post-work summary inside Salesforce.
  • Oracle Fusion Field Service: a start-of-day brief that reserves parts, and draft work orders.
  • ServiceNow Field Service Management: work orders from text and images, and forms filled from photos.
  • Simpro Lightning: the job written up from the technician talking.

Before the technician leaves

The brief is the most common use. Salesforce drafts a pre-work brief. IFS gives a work briefing before the job. SAP sends each technician a daily briefing by email. Oracle's start-of-day brief also reserves the parts.

A brief saves reading, not thinking. It summarises what is already in the system, so it cannot tell the technician anything the records do not hold.

On site

This is where the products differ most. Aquant guides the technician by voice, text or the phone camera. Neuron7 answers inside the CRM or field service system. Kenner answers by message or phone call from the history of the unit in front of the technician.

Photos and voice are now normal inputs. ServiceNow creates work orders from text and images and fills forms from photos. Salesforce's assistant can read photos.

After the job

Almost every suite drafts the summary. The difference is the input. Simpro Lightning and Kenner write the job up from the technician speaking. Microsoft updates the work order from speech or text, in preview.

The report is also next year's answer. A summary that records what was found and what fixed it is what the troubleshooting tools later read.

In the office

Scheduling and dispatch optimisation sits in the suites. Analytics is split: Aquant reports on teams, assets and parts, and Kenner reports what breaks across units and which faults take longest to diagnose.

Parts and quotes are the newest use. Oracle reserves parts in its brief, and Kenner orders parts and prepares quotes through the CRM and ERP.

Questions and answers

Which use case should we start with?

The one tied to a number you already track. If technicians lose time on paperwork, start with the report. If second visits are the problem, start with troubleshooting.

Does AI do predictive maintenance in field service?

Some suites market it. The pages we checked for these twelve products describe briefs, guidance, summaries and scheduling, so we do not make claims about prediction here.
